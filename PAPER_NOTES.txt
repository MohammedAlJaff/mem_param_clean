001 - BACK TO BASICS: GENERATION AND MEMORIZATION
Added: 2026-09-07
Status: Draft framing. The proposed comparison still needs evidence.
Use in: Introduction, related work, or discussion.

DRAFT PARAGRAPH
Li and He (2025) show that predicting clean data can make large-patch pixel
Transformers work where noise prediction fails. We ask what happens when both
work. Does predicting clean data or noise change when a model starts to
memorize, and how much it memorizes? We study this question by tracking sample
quality and memorization together under matched conditions.

SHORT FORM
When both methods generate useful samples, does what they predict change what
they memorize?

WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS
The reported failure concerns large-patch pixel Transformers. Resolution alone
is not the explanation: token dimension and network capacity matter too. The
paper also notes that low-dimensional latents make noise prediction easier.
Our question adds memorization to this picture.

IDEA TO TEST
Use one fixed autoencoder and compare x-, eps-, and v-prediction in its latent
space. Here x means the clean latent. A 32 x 32 x 4 latent is one possible
setting, with 4,096 values. Confirm its shape and scaling for the chosen encoder.
This experiment is proposed; it has not been validated here. A pixel-space
setting where all methods work could answer the same question.

WHAT WE NEED TO CHECK
- Both methods must generate useful samples. Low loss alone does not show this.
  Failed generation with low f_mem is not evidence of better generalization.
- Keep data, architecture, optimizer, training budget, and sampling settings
  matched. Record any differences.
- Separate the prediction target from the loss. Compare x/v, eps/v, and v/v
  under a common velocity loss as well as the diagonal objectives.
- Track quality and memorization across checkpoints and seeds. Compare fixed
  training budgets and comparable quality, with a stated checkpoint rule.
- Keep any autoencoder fixed. Record its source and reconstruction quality.
  Check decoded samples against the saved training and held-out images.
- Let the results establish which method memorizes more. Do not assume an order.

CORE QUESTION
Under matched data, architecture, optimizer, sampler, and evaluation, how do
prediction and loss parameterizations change the route to memorization?

The current evidence supports a timing claim. It does not yet support a final
multi-seed parameterization ranking. The online monitor uses only 128 samples
in a random projection of resized pixels. Final paper claims require fixed-10k
FID, full-pixel f_mem, learned-feature f_mem, and multiple seeds.

Direct-safe and via-x-safe EPS/EPS are close. Direct-safe is better at 100k
and 390k. Via-x-safe is 0.49 FID better at 695k. This small late difference is
not a stable scientific ordering.

Exact EPS/EPS sampling fails badly early. Exact full-Heun FID is 440.13 at
100k, 176.95 at 390k, and 79.54 at 695k. At 100k, 85.77% of raw pixels lie
outside [-1,1]. Safe division is therefore required for robust generation in
the current endpoint range. Exact division remains a diagnostic.

The saved x/x and v/v references used final Euler, while the primary EPS/EPS
run used full Heun without final Euler. Their checkpoints are useful, but the
paper must replay all compared parameterizations with one common sampler
before treating the FID difference as a parameterization effect.

The five standard-learning-rate EPS/EPS jobs mainly change monitoring-time
sampling. They are not independent training seeds. Do not report them as
replicates.

The controlled pairs show three effects. RGB slows memorization at 32x32.

CROSS-STUDY FINDINGS THAT THE CURRENT EVIDENCE SUPPORTS

1. Prediction and loss parameterization change when memorization appears.
   The 3x3 trajectories separate strongly before many endpoints saturate.

2. Sample quality and memorization are distinct axes.
   x/eps gives coherent faces with lower f_mem. eps/x gives noisier faces with
   high f_mem. Failed Flowers models also have low f_mem, but this is not useful
   generalization.

3. A parameterization comparison is valid only in a useful-generation regime.
   The 64x64 studies show that insufficient capacity can dominate the outcome.

4. Safe EPS/EPS sampling is an empirical requirement in the current endpoint
   range. Exact division is a diagnostic, not the primary paper sampler.

5. Optional attention is secondary to width and depth in the seed-zero 64x64
   matrix. A stable attention claim still needs multiple seeds.

6. Endpoint-only reporting hides the main effect. The paper must show
   checkpoint trajectories and quality beside memorization.

CLAIMS THAT ARE NOT YET SUPPORTED

- No final ordering across x, eps, and v is established across seeds.
- The off-diagonal 3x3 ordering has not been tested with fixed-10k metrics.
- The projected-pixel ordering has not been confirmed in SSCD, LPIPS, or
  DINOv2 space.
- No 64x64 parameterization comparison exists. The 64x64 matrix is EPS/EPS
  only and mainly diagnoses capacity and sampling.
- Attention does not have a confirmed population effect.
- The 1M endpoints have not received the corrected fixed-10k evaluation.

Added: 2026-09-21
Status: Current working abstract before the full multi-seed evaluation.
Use in: Manuscript planning. Revise after T019 and T020 are complete.

This version uses natural research-paper prose. It describes the current
evidence without claiming a stable ordering. It treats multi-seed and learned-
feature results as future validation rather than completed findings.

LATEX

\begin{abstract}
Flow matching models trained on finite data can memorize their training
examples, but model size, training time, and model design can delay or limit
this behavior. We ask whether target parameterization---the choice to predict
clean data $\vx$, noise $\veps$, or velocity $\vv$---also changes when and how
memorization develops. We study this question in controlled image-generation
experiments. We vary both what the model predicts and how its training loss is
defined. Throughout training, we measure sample quality and how closely
generated images match individual training examples. We also examine changes
in dataset, image size, color, and model size. Our experiments indicate that
what the model predicts and how its loss is defined can alter when memorization
appears and how it grows, even when the models produce recognizable samples.
Sample quality and memorization do not always move together. In more demanding
settings, models that are too small can fail to produce coherent images, making
memorization results hard to interpret. These observations suggest that target
parameterization shapes not only generation quality but also the path to
memorization. They motivate broader tests across random seeds and different
ways of comparing images.
\end{abstract}
